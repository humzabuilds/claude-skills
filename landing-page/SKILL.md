# landing-page: start from the best, then beat it

AI gives everyone the same landing page: a gradient, a vague headline, three icons and a button. This skill starts from the best page in the industry and does not stop until yours is clearly better.

## 1. Get the basics (ask only what you cannot find)
- What is being sold, to whom, and the one action the page wants (book, buy, sign up, call).
- The price or offer, if there is one.
- Real proof: reviews, numbers, logos, photos. Never invent any of these. If there is none, leave a clearly marked placeholder.

## 2. Find the best site in the industry
Search for the top 3 to 5 pages in this niche (the companies that clearly win: well known, well reviewed, clearly spending on marketing). Pick the strongest one. Write down why it works:
- the headline and what promise it makes
- what you see before scrolling
- how fast it gets to proof
- how many steps to the main action
- what it does on a phone

This is the bar. The page you build must beat it.

## 3. Build the page
- **Headline:** one clear outcome for the customer, in plain words. No "Welcome to", no slogans that could fit any business.
- **Above the fold:** the headline, one line that backs it up, the main button, and one piece of proof.
- **Proof early:** reviews, numbers or photos within the first scroll.
- **One main action** repeated down the page. Not five different buttons.
- **Mobile first.** Big tap targets, short lines, nothing that needs side scrolling.
- **Real words from the customer's side,** not industry jargon.
- **Fast:** no huge images or heavy effects that slow the page down.

## 4. Compare side by side
Render both pages (theirs and yours) at phone size (375 px wide) and laptop size (1440 px wide). Take screenshots and put them next to each other. If you cannot take screenshots, open both and compare section by section.

For each of these, decide who wins and write it down:
1. Headline: which is clearer about the outcome?
2. First screen: which makes you want to keep going?
3. Proof: which shows it sooner and more convincingly?
4. Main button: which is easier to find and tap?
5. Phone: which reads better with a thumb?
6. Speed and clutter: which feels lighter?
7. Trust: which looks more like a real, established business?

## 5. Fix every spot where yours is not clearly better
You are not grading your page on its own. You are grading it against the best. A tie is a loss. For each item where the competitor wins or ties, fix your page, re-render and compare again. Repeat until yours wins every row.

## 6. Hand it over
Show the final side by side screenshots on phone and laptop, the scorecard with every row won, and a list of placeholders that need real content (photos, reviews, numbers). Never present invented reviews or numbers as real.
