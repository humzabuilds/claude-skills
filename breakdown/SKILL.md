# breakdown: keep splitting until every step can actually be done

## What this replaces
The five bullet points called a plan. The six steps that each hide twenty. The estimate that says "about $200 a month" with no unit and no source. If a step cannot be handed to one person and checked off with proof, it is not finished being broken down.

## Step 1. Map the angles before you split anything
Before writing a single step, decide how the subject can be cut. Depth on the first angle you think of is not detail, it is one opinion with indentation. Detail comes from crossing angles.

1. **Name the smallest real unit.** One click, one dollar, one email, one form field, one minute of someone's time. Everything is measured against it.
2. **Walk the angle list below** and note which ones matter for this subject.
3. **Pick 2 to 4 angles and cross them.** Steps crossed with who does them. Costs crossed with volume. Parts crossed with what can go wrong. Crossing is where the hidden steps fall out.
4. **Say how deep you will go and why you stop there.**
5. **Set the stop rule now** (Step 4), before you get tired.

Show this map to the user first, in a few lines, then build the breakdown.

### The angle list (check all of them, use the ones that matter)
1. **Parts:** what it is made of.
2. **Order:** what happens first, next, last, and what waits on what.
3. **Who:** the person, tool, vendor or customer doing each step.
4. **States:** every state it can be in, including stuck and broken ones.
5. **Information:** what each step needs to know and where that comes from.
6. **Handoffs:** every point where work passes between people or tools.
7. **Money:** the cost per unit, not per month in general.
8. **Time:** one-time versus ongoing, busy week versus quiet week.
9. **Scale:** the same plan at 1x, 10x and 100x. What breaks first.
10. **Variations:** different customers, plans, devices, locations.
11. **Dependencies:** what must exist first, and what is outside your control.
12. **Failure:** for each step, what breaks it and what happens next.
13. **Proof:** how each step is shown to be done, and by whom.
14. **Confidence:** which facts are checked and which are guesses.
15. **Rules:** terms, legal, tax, platform limits.
16. **Approval:** who decides and who signs off.
17. **Hidden work:** setup time, retries, cleanup, support, the thing nobody lists.

## Step 2. Split, then split again
At every item ask three questions. Each new answer becomes a sub-step.
1. What is this made of?
2. What has to happen, in what order, and who does it?
3. What does it cost, how can it fail, and how do we prove it is done?

An item is finished only when all five are true:
- **One owner.**
- **One action or one number.**
- **One thing you can open to see it:** a file, a link, a screenshot, a receipt, a row.
- **One way to check it.**
- **Splitting it further would mean making things up.**

Go at least 3 levels below the original ask. Spend the most depth on the most expensive or riskiest branch, not the easiest one to write.

## Step 3. Label every number
- **[CHECKED]** you looked it up. Say where and when.
- **[MATH]** you calculated it. Show the formula and its inputs.
- **[GUESS]** you assumed it. Give low, likely and high, and say what would prove it wrong.

A number with no label is a mistake. Never carry a number from memory into a fresh breakdown without checking it again.

## Step 4. When to stop
Stop a branch only when:
- every item in it passes the five checks above, or
- splitting further would change no decision and no number by more than about 2 percent (or one hour of work).

When you stop for the second reason, write the rolled-up number with a range and list the branch under "Not covered". Never drop a branch silently. If you cut a list to the top few, say how many you cut.

## Step 5. Costs (when the question is "what does this cost")
- Find the real unit you pay for, then go one level finer.
- Give each cost line: vendor, unit, quantity, unit price, total, billing period, label and source.
- Check for hidden costs: setup time, your own hours, retries, failed attempts, refunds, support, tools you already pay for twice.
- Show three columns: the minimum just to exist, the expected case (state the volume), and the worst believable case.
- Make the lines add up to the total at every level, with the math shown.
- Never spend money just to find out a price.

## Step 6. The output
Deliver a document in this order:
1. The ask and what done looks like.
2. The angle map from Step 1.
3. The breakdown as a numbered tree (1, 1.1, 1.1.1). Keep the numbers stable if you update it later.
4. A table of every final item: `ID | step | owner | unit | qty | cost | label | source | how to check | status`
5. The totals, with the math.
6. Guesses, with their ranges.
7. Not covered.
8. Next actions, each with an owner.

## Final check before you hand it over
1. Angle map came before the breakdown.
2. 2 to 4 angles actually crossed.
3. Every final item passes the five checks.
4. At least 3 levels deep, deepest where it matters most.
5. Every number labelled.
6. Lines add up to totals.
7. Every stop explained, nothing dropped silently.
8. Every item has an owner and a way to check it.
9. What is not covered is written down.
10. It is a table someone can act on, not a wall of text.

## Do not
- Hand over a first-level list as if it were detailed.
- Use one angle (usually "steps in order") and call it complete.
- Pad with fake sub-steps of suspiciously equal size.
- Use round numbers with no source ("a few hundred", "several").
- Go deep on the easy part and write one line for the hard part.
